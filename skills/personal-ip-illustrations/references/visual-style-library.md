# Visual Style Library

Use this library to avoid repeating the same "center machine with left input and right output" layout. Pick one visual mode before writing the prompt. Keep the user's boy IP and Bichon companion fixed across all modes.

Do not copy the original Xiaohei examples exactly. Reuse the visual grammar, not the specific composition.

## 1. Machine Operator

Best for workflows, judgment, processing, automation, and content production.

Visual grammar:
- one low-tech machine in the center;
- inputs on one side, outputs on the other;
- boy inside or beside the machine operating a lever, crank, switch, or gauge;
- Bichon pushes or fetches one input item.

Avoid using this as the default every time.

## 2. Living Filter / Sorting Body

Best for prioritization, filtering, segmentation, and "先判断再行动".

Visual grammar:
- the character or an object acts as a funnel, sieve, pocket, drawer, or sorter;
- 2-3 outputs emerge as cards;
- short labels describe destination or purpose;
- Bichon nudges a card into the correct slot.

For the personal IP, avoid turning the boy into a weird body-object too often. Prefer a wearable apron, sorting cape, large pocket, or badminton bag as the filter.

## 3. Big Object Metaphor

Best for "one material becomes many uses", repurposing, content assets, and transformation.

Visual grammar:
- one memorable oversized object occupies the left or center;
- the boy edits, cuts, folds, stamps, or measures it;
- outputs are small cards or objects drifting to the side;
- Bichon holds a tiny offcut, marker, or shuttlecock.

Personal IP object ideas:
- oversized shuttlecock;
- paper ball;
- folded towel;
- sports bag;
- giant sticky note;
- soft white ball guarded by the Bichon.

## 4. Curved Path / Handoff Route

Best for onboarding, learning paths, handoff, trust building, funnel movement, and journeys.

Visual grammar:
- one loose orange route crosses the page;
- 3-5 sparse landmarks sit along the route;
- boy pulls, repairs, or walks the route;
- Bichon carries a marker, guards a node, or fetches a dropped card;
- optional blue dashed return loop.

Keep landmarks small. The route should breathe.

## 5. Layered Bucket / Multi-Source Stack

Best for multiple inputs, source consolidation, research, signal collection, and "都要进来".

Visual grammar:
- a stacked cylinder, bucket, shelf, or tiered tray;
- 3 input layers or sources;
- one output bucket or tray;
- boy pulls strings, opens valves, or checks levels;
- Bichon sits at the bottom catching useful pieces.

Use orange for downward or converging movement.

## 6. Branching Jobs

Best for one artifact with multiple purposes, content distribution, role separation, or "别混用".

Visual grammar:
- one source object on the left;
- 3 branches flow to 3 tiny scenes on the right;
- each tiny scene shows an action, not just a label;
- boy may appear once at the source or as small repeated simplified poses;
- Bichon should appear once as a connector or helper, not repeated in every branch.

Limit to 3 branches.

## 7. Toolbox / Stamp Kit

Best for methods, reusable scripts, templates, handoff copy, checklists, and operational assets.

Visual grammar:
- open toolbox, stamp kit, drawer, tray, or small workbench;
- boy chooses or applies one tool;
- Bichon fetches a stamp, card, or small part;
- before/after cards sit on either side.

This mode can have a slightly more centered object but keep the labels sparse.

## 8. Pitfall Road / Risk Holes

Best for common mistakes, failure points, warnings, decision traps, and anti-patterns.

Visual grammar:
- an orange path crosses several holes, cracks, puddles, gates, or warning signs;
- each problem has a very short red label;
- boy avoids, patches, or marks the danger;
- Bichon pulls the boy away, carries a warning flag, or guards a safe route.

Do not make it scary; keep it dry and deadpan.

## 9. Information Well / Deep Container

Best for noise, overload, retrieval, search, first-principle picking, and "从一堆里捞一个".

Visual grammar:
- one deep well, drawer, basket, or pile;
- many tiny scraps inside, drawn lightly;
- boy uses a rope, net, hook, or racket to retrieve one useful item;
- Bichon holds the bucket, rope end, or selected item.

Use more internal detail than usual, but leave the outside canvas clean.

## 10. Fermentation Jar / Time Capsule

Best for incubation, long-term asset building, slow thinking, compounding, and reuse.

Visual grammar:
- one large transparent jar, bottle, box, or capsule;
- ideas or notes swirl inside;
- boy records, pumps, seals, or feeds the container;
- Bichon watches the output shelf or fetches the finished block.

Orange can show circular motion or slow transformation.

## 11. Load-Bearing Contrast

Best for pain point -> system support, manual burden -> mechanism, repeated work, delegation, and process design.

Visual grammar:
- left side: messy pressure or overload;
- right side: a simple mechanism carrying the same load;
- boy adjusts the mechanism or releases a lever;
- Bichon brings a small tool or holds the safety line.

Use this when the article says "不要靠人硬扛".

## 12. Trust Bridge / Evidence Path

Best for trust, conversion, proof, relationship warming, and from stranger to willing action.

Visual grammar:
- two islands, platforms, doors, or rooms;
- small evidence cards bridge the gap;
- boy slowly lays cards or checks the bridge;
- Bichon carries one card across or tests the bridge with one paw.

Keep it quiet. This mode benefits from lots of white space.

## Selection Heuristic

- If the idea is about converting inputs to outputs: Machine Operator or Toolbox.
- If the idea is about choosing or prioritizing: Living Filter or Information Well.
- If the idea is about a journey: Curved Path or Trust Bridge.
- If the idea is about many sources: Layered Bucket.
- If the idea is about one thing serving many goals: Branching Jobs or Big Object Metaphor.
- If the idea is about mistakes: Pitfall Road.
- If the idea is about compounding over time: Fermentation Jar.
- If the idea is about reducing burden: Load-Bearing Contrast.
